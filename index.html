Who We Are

Philly Moved In.
The South Side Ate.

BoPhilly is a counter-serve corner spot on the 95th St corridor doing one thing the way it's supposed to be done: the Philly cheesesteak — thin-cut steak hitting a screaming-hot griddle, chopped down with the spatula, folded into cheese, and packed into a roll while it's still steaming.

No gimmicks, no fifteen-page menu, no ambiance to speak of. Just a 4.7-star reputation built sandwich by sandwich, regular by regular — the kind of word-of-mouth you can't buy.

The rule of the house:

If it doesn't drip, it doesn't leave the counter. Every steak is griddled and chopped to order — nothing sitting under a heat lamp, ever.

How a Real One Gets Made

Hit the Flat-Top

Thin-cut steak lands on a hot griddle. That sizzle you hear from the doorway? That's yours.

Folded into Whiz, provolone, or American, then packed into the roll hot. Wrapped tight. Gone in minutes.
